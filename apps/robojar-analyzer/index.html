Statistics Reference

Use these definitions to interpret the statistics card and compare RoboJar runs confidently.

Count, Min & Max

Count is the number of samples within the selected window. Min and Max capture the lowest and highest readings observed.

Mean & Median

The mean is the arithmetic average. The median marks the middle of the sorted data and is less sensitive to sudden spikes.

Standard Deviation (Std Dev)

Average distance between each reading and the mean. Larger values indicate greater variability; smaller values signal a tighter, steadier process.

Coefficient of Variation (CV %)

Standard deviation expressed as a percentage of the mean. Compare stability between runs regardless of scale. CV below about 15% is typically very stable.

P10 & P90

10th and 90th percentile values. Most readings fall inside this band, defining the typical operating window for the run.

Interquartile Range (IQR)

Difference between the 75th and 25th percentiles. Highlights the spread of the middle half of the data while filtering out extreme outliers.

Noise Indicator

A quick visual cue driven by CV %. Green means low noise, amber is moderate, and red suggests highly variable readings that may need attention.

Forecast Quality Metrics

These measures show how closely a forecast follows the historical RoboJar data. Review them whenever forecasting is enabled to confirm the prediction is trustworthy.

R² (Fit Quality)

Ranges from 0 to 1. Values near 1 mean the model explains almost all variation in the data. Scores below about 0.7 suggest the fit may be weak.

RMSE (Root Mean Squared Error)

Expressed in the same units as the chart. It highlights larger errors, so lower values indicate the forecast stays close to observed readings.

MAE (Mean Absolute Error)

The average absolute difference between predicted and observed values. Less sensitive to isolated spikes than RMSE, but also best when small.

MAPE (Mean Absolute Percentage Error)

Shows the typical error relative to the data value. Under 10% is excellent, 10-25% is acceptable, and above 25% indicates the forecast likely needs review.
